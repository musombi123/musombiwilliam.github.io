How research is conducted

Every project should leave an inspectable trail from question to evidence and implementation.

Define the problem

State the question, intended users, constraints, assumptions and what would count as a useful result.

Review existing work

Identify relevant prior art, technical documentation, standards and known limitations before building.

Design an experiment

Choose measurable criteria, test data, controls and a repeatable procedure. Protect private or sensitive information.

Build and test

Implement a prototype, run tests, record failures and compare results against the stated criteria.

Verify and document

Separate observations from interpretation. Publish methods, evidence, limitations and reproducibility instructions where appropriate.

Improve responsibly

Use review and new evidence to refine the work. Do not present an experiment or prototype as a validated production system.

Research principles

Evidence over claims

Performance, safety and capability statements require testable evidence. A successful demo is not proof of production readiness.

Reproducibility

Record versions, configurations, procedures and test conditions so another person can inspect or repeat the work.

Responsible collaboration

Respect intellectual contributions, privacy, licensing, consent and security boundaries. Only conduct security testing with authorization.
